## Privacy and Encryption

 

AFTERTONE uses browser-based cryptography intended to protect message content during communication.

 

Encryption is a security measure, not a guarantee of complete anonymity or absolute protection against every form of attack.

 

The server and hosting infrastructure may process technical information associated with requests, connections, and service operation. Recipients may also retain information they receive.

 

The key exchange uses ephemeral P-256 ECDH and an HMAC-SHA256 key confirmation, but it is not authenticated as to identity. Both participants must compare the displayed fingerprint through a trusted channel and press VERIFY before messaging is enabled; a mismatched, stale, or one-sided verification does not enable messages, and each client requires the peer's key confirmation to validate first. Verification and key confirmation do not prove the peer's identity. If the fingerprint is not compared, an active attacker controlling the relay may be able to substitute keys and interfere with the connection.

 

Each encrypted application message carries an authenticated exchange identifier, sender, recipient, and per-sender sequence number. The receiving client rejects duplicate, reordered, stale-session, and cross-exchange messages after decryption. This reduces replay risk within a session; it does not prevent a relay from suppressing or delaying traffic.
